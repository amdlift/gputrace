# Close the `dlopen` escape by interposing `dlsym`

ADR-0003 closed two resolution paths and claimed no call path escaped capture. A third does: `dlsym` on a handle from `dlopen("libcuda.so.1")` searches that object alone, so `LD_PRELOAD` has nothing to shadow and the dispatch-table hook is never asked. Measuring real stacks (`docs/dlopen-escape.md`) found this is not a corner but the main road — it is how the CUDA Runtime and cuBLASLt reach the driver, and a stock `cudaMalloc`/`cudaMemcpy` program under `gputrace capture` produces no bundle at all while doing real work on the GPU.

We will **export our own `dlsym` (and `dlvsym`) and answer `cu*` lookups with our trampolines**, leaving every other name a pass-through. One hook covers both mechanisms: the Runtime's lookup of `cuGetProcAddress_v2` comes back as our dispatch hook, which ADR-0003's machinery already handles, and a direct lookup of `cuLaunchKernel` comes back as the shim. All of it is resolution-time work, so ADR-0004's record path is untouched. The alternative we keep in reserve is `LD_AUDIT`, whose `la_objsearch` can redirect the target's search for `libcuda.so.1` to our object — cleaner in principle, worse in practice, and glibc-only. Doing nothing was rejected: the stacks that escape are the stacks the project exists to capture. `docs/dlopen-escape.md` carries the evidence and the full cost comparison; the implementation and its hazards (a hot startup path, and our own resolver re-entering the hook) are #15.
